# Literature

What was already known before this study, who established it, on what data, and what it leaves open.

## 1. What financial well-being is

Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. (2015). *Financial well-being: The goal of financial education*. https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/201501_cfpb_report_financial-well-being.pdf

Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. (2017). *CFPB Financial Well-Being Scale: Scale development technical report*. https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/201705_cfpb_financial-well-being-scale-technical-report.pdf

The definition behind our outcome: control over day-to-day finances, capacity to absorb a shock, being on track to meet goals, and freedom to make choices that allow enjoyment of life. The ten-item scale is scored as a single number, with separate scoring for adults 62 and older.

Brüggen, E. C., Hogreve, J., Holmlund, M., Kabadayi, S., & Löfgren, M. (2017). Financial well-being: A conceptualization and research agenda. *Journal of Business Research*, 79, 228–237. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2017.03.013

Defines financial well-being as a perception, so people in the same objective position can assess it differently. Their research agenda calls for studying the mismatch between objective and subjective indicators, which is the question this project takes up.

Netemeyer, R. G., Warmath, D., Fernandes, D., & Lynch, J. G., Jr. (2018). How am I doing? Perceived financial well-being, its potential antecedents, and its relation to overall well-being. *Journal of Consumer Research*, 45(1), 68–89. https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucx109

Financial well-being has two related components: current money management stress and expected future financial security. Their own scales, U.S. national samples.

Riitsalu, L., & van Raaij, W. F. (2022). Current and future financial well-being in 16 countries. *Journal of International Marketing*, 30(3), 35–56. https://doi.org/10.1177/1069031X221095076

Current and future financial well-being have different antecedents; income predicts both. The authors argue that interventions should target one component or the other.

## 2. The theory

Thaler, R. H., & Shefrin, H. M. (1981). An economic theory of self-control. *Journal of Political Economy*, 89(2), 392–406. https://doi.org/10.1086/260971

Shefrin, H. M., & Thaler, R. H. (1988). The behavioral life-cycle hypothesis. *Economic Inquiry*, 26(4), 609–643. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1465-7295.1988.tb01520.x

People act as if a far-sighted "planner" and a present-focused "doer" are in conflict, so financial outcomes depend on the ability to control impulses and the cost of doing so. This is the reason to expect self-discipline and planning horizon to matter for financial well-being.

## 3. Self-control and financial well-being

Strömbäck, C., Lind, T., Skagerlund, K., Västfjäll, D., & Tinghög, G. (2017). Does self-control predict financial behavior and financial well-being? *Journal of Behavioral and Experimental Finance*, 14, 30–38. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbef.2017.04.002

Self-control is associated with lower financial anxiety and greater perceived financial security. 2,063 Swedish adults; OLS; behavioral life-cycle framework.

Leaves open: the only economic control is three income bands, so savings, hardship, and credit problems are not held constant. Their own well-being scales.

van Raaij, W. F., Riitsalu, L., & Põder, K. (2023). Direct and indirect effects of self-control and future time perspective on financial well-being. *Journal of Economic Psychology*, 99, 102667. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joep.2023.102667 (open access; data on OSF)

Self-control is the main determinant of current money management stress; future time perspective is the main determinant of expected future security. ING International Survey on Savings, 2019; 15,773 adults in 16 countries, including 1,034 in the United States; structural equation modeling. One of their self-control items did not correlate with the others.

Leaves open: a different well-being measure from the CFPB scale; no test of predictive value.

Netemeyer et al. (2018), above, also bears directly on this question. Lack of self-control predicted current money management stress but not expected future security.

## 4. How self-control is measured

Hagger, M. S., Zhang, C.-Q., Kangro, E.-M., Ries, F., Wang, J. C. K., Heritage, B., & Chan, D. K. C. (2021). Trait self-control and self-discipline: Structure, validity, and invariance across national groups. *Current Psychology*, 40(3), 1015–1030. https://doi.org/10.1007/s12144-018-0021-6 (open access)

The Brief Self-Control Scale has two distinct components, restraint and non-impulsivity, and the two predict different behaviors. 1,282 students in four national groups. This is the basis for keeping the survey's impulsivity item separate from the two restraint items rather than averaging all three.

## 5. Planning horizon

Netemeyer et al. (2018) and van Raaij et al. (2023), above, both link a long-term orientation to expected future financial security.

Hudson, C. R., Copeland, C., & Young, J. (2022). Financial well-being: Psychological factors that affect African Americans' financial well-being. *Journal of Financial Therapy*, 13(2), 4. https://doi.org/10.4148/1944-9771.1296

Uses this survey's planning-horizon item. Short-term planners were less likely to have high financial well-being among White and Hispanic/other respondents; the association was not significant among African Americans (n = 407). 4,329 respondents with household income of $40,000 or more; multinomial logit.

Leaves open: horizon split into two groups, financial well-being cut into three bins, and no controls for savings, hardship, or credit problems.

## 6. This survey, already studied

Abt Associates. (2018). *Understanding the pathways to financial well-being. National Financial Well-Being Survey: Report 2*. Prepared for the Bureau of Consumer Financial Protection. Authors: J. T. Walker, D. G. Bocian, D. DeMarco, & B. Freeman.

The CFPB's own analysis of this survey. Objective financial situation has by far the strongest association with financial well-being. Self-control, entered as a control, keeps a small positive coefficient after objective financial situation (standardized 0.05). Structural equation models; full survey.

Leaves open: self-control is the average of all three items, which do not hold together as a scale; the planning-horizon item is not used; the traits are background controls, so their added contribution and predictive value are not examined.

Collins, J. M., & Urban, C. (2020). Measuring financial well-being over the lifecourse. *The European Journal of Finance*, 26(4–5), 341–359. https://doi.org/10.1080/1351847X.2019.1682631

Financial well-being rises with income, savings, and age, and measured financial knowledge has almost no association with it once economic resources are held constant. Stepwise weighted OLS on this survey; code published on GitHub.

Leaves open: no self-control or planning-horizon measures.

## 7. What we claim, and what we do not

We do not claim:

- to have shown that self-control is associated with financial well-being after objective circumstances on this survey — Abt Associates (2018)
- to have shown that financial knowledge fades once economic resources are held constant — Collins & Urban (2020)
- to have identified present and future components of financial well-being, or that their antecedents differ — Netemeyer et al. (2018); Riitsalu & van Raaij (2022); van Raaij et al. (2023)
- to have shown that self-control has separate restraint and impulsivity components — Hagger et al. (2021)
- that self-discipline or planning horizon causes higher financial well-being — the data are cross-sectional, and current circumstances may themselves reflect earlier self-discipline

What appears to remain open:

- **The item split on this survey.** The CFPB's analysis averaged the three self-control items. We did not find work on this survey that separates impulsivity from self-discipline.
- **Planning horizon with full controls.** The one study using the horizon item on this survey dichotomized it and did not hold savings, hardship, or credit problems constant.
- **How much the traits add.** Prior analyses of this survey treat the traits as controls; we measure the variation they explain beyond objective circumstances.
- **Prediction.** None of the studies reviewed tests whether these traits improve out-of-sample prediction of an individual's financial well-being.
- **The pattern by component.** Whether impulsivity and self-discipline relate differently to present and future well-being on the CFPB scale.
